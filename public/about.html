About NU Map

NU Map is an independent, student-built course planner. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or officially connected to Northeastern University.

Northeastern already has tools for your past and your present. There is nothing for your future.

The degree audit your past the authoritative checklist: your requirements, and what you've completed against them
Banner your present live registration, the most up-to-date course information
NU Map your future the semesters you haven't taken yet

To be fair, the audit does list what you still need. But it's built to verify, not to plan: it checks your record against the requirements and tells you what's missing. It can't tell you what order to take things in, when they're actually offered, or how any of it fits around co-op. Nobody official will look at your next four years and tell you if they actually work. That's what NU Map is for.

Why we built it

Planning meant coordinating way too many sources. The catalog for descriptions, Banner for offerings, the audit for requirements, another site for professors, and a homemade spreadsheet holding it all together. Dozens of spreadsheets, hundreds of webpages. Just collecting everything into one list took hours.

The data exists, but it's a pain to get to. Banner and the audit technically have everything. But you have to log in every time, click into each course one by one, and the UI is not exactly intuitive. When you just want to sit down and think about your plan, that's a lot of friction.

You can't see the prereqs. Say you finally get every class you need lined up in a spreadsheet. Now try putting them in order. You can't see the prerequisites! How are you supposed to figure out what has to come before what? And even if you somehow get the ordering right, you still have no idea if a course is actually offered in the spring, or whether it fills up. NU Map shows the chains, checks your plan as you build it, and shows when each course actually ran and how full it got.

Co-op scrambles everything. Your semesters don't just alternate fall and spring like the sample plans assume. A sequence that works on paper falls apart when the middle course lands during your co-op. In NU Map, co-op blocks are part of the plan.

What-ifs meant starting over. "What if I add the minor?" "What if I do three co-ops?" In a spreadsheet, that's a full copy and a redo. In NU Map it's just another plan.

Plans get shared. With roommates, with upperclassmen, with your advisor, and advising meetings are short. So NU Map shares a plan as a link, a short code, or a PDF.

What NU Map is not

NU Map is NOT trying to replace Banner. Banner is for live registration and has THE most up-to-date course information. It is not trying to replace the degree audit either, which is the MOST authoritative source for your program requirements. And it is not a replacement for your advisor. What NU Map replaces is the Excel spreadsheet people use to plan into the future. Plan here, register on Banner, verify with the audit and your advisor.

Philosophy

NU Map trusts you. It will basically let you do anything, because real degrees are full of special circumstances: substitutions, placed-out courses, transfer credit, AP/IB, things your advisor approved that no tool could know about. It warns you when something looks off, but it doesn't stop you.

Everything stays in your browser. Your plans and anything you enter never touch a server. No account, no tracking, no ads. The privacy policy has the details.

It's open. The code is on GitHub and the course and program data is published at numap.app/data, so anyone can check our work.

And it's free. Hand-translated into eight languages, works on your phone, costs nothing.

The story

NU Map is built by two brothers at Northeastern who plan more than is probably healthy. Before this existed, we did it the manual way: going through every past semester one at a time to figure out when courses actually run, and keeping it all lined up in spreadsheets. The spreadsheet couldn't see prereqs, didn't know about co-op, and checked nothing. Eventually we built the tool we wished we had.
