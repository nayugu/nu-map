About NU Map

NU Map is an independent, student-built course planner. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or officially connected to Northeastern University.

Every tool Northeastern gives you covers a different tense:

The degree audit your past the authoritative record of what you've completed
Banner your present live registration, this term's real seats and sections
NU Map your future every semester you haven't taken yet

Nothing official will look at your next four years and tell you whether they work. That gap is what NU Map fills: a place to plan every future semester — around co-op — with prerequisite chains, offering history, and degree requirements checked as you go. One tab, no login, free.

The problems it targets

Too many tabs and spreadsheets. Planning used to mean the catalog, the schedule, the audit, professor reviews, and a homemade spreadsheet — dozens of tabs, hundreds of clicks, all coordinated by hand. NU Map puts the catalog, requirements, offering history, and professors in the same view.

The data exists, but it's locked up. Banner and the audit technically know everything — behind a login and a 2FA prompt, one click-in page at a time. When you just want to think, that friction kills the thinking. NU Map opens in a second and asks you for nothing.

You can't see prerequisite chains. Line your courses up in a spreadsheet and the hard part hasn't started: which has to come before which? A spreadsheet can't see prereqs, doesn't know a course only runs in fall, and can't tell you it filled up last spring. NU Map shows the chain, checks it as you drag, and shows when each course actually ran and how full it got.

Co-op breaks every sequence. This is the Northeastern problem: your semesters don't alternate neatly, and a chain that works on paper falls apart when its middle link lands during your co-op. NU Map treats co-op blocks as part of the plan, so you thread sequences through your real pattern, not an idealized one.

What-ifs are expensive. "What if I add the minor? Switch concentrations? Go five years with three co-ops?" In a spreadsheet that's a full copy and a redo; officially it's an advising appointment. In NU Map it's another named plan, made and discarded in a minute.

Planning is social. Plans get compared with roommates, handed down from upperclassmen, and brought to 15-minute advising meetings. NU Map shares a plan as a snapshot link, a short pickup code, or a PDF — so the meeting starts at the plan, not at a blank audit.

What NU Map is not

NU Map does not replace Banner, which is for live registration and is the most current course information. It does not replace the degree audit, which is the authoritative record of your requirements and what you've completed. It replaces the spreadsheet — the unofficial thing students actually use to plan ahead. Verify every plan with your academic advisor.

Principles

Trust the student. NU Map will let you do almost anything, because real degrees are full of special circumstances: substitutions, placed-out courses, transfer and AP/IB credit, overrides an advisor approved. The tool accommodates your situation instead of arguing with it — it warns, it never blocks.

Your plan is yours. Plans, grades, everything you enter lives in your browser and never touches a server. No account, no tracking, no ads. The full details are in the privacy policy — it's short because there's little to disclose.

Open by default. The code is open source and the course and program data is published openly — for people and for AI assistants — at numap.app/data. You can check our work.

For everyone. The whole planner is hand-translated into eight languages, works on phones, and costs nothing.

The story

NU Map is built by two brothers studying at Northeastern who are, frankly, obsessive planners. Before this existed, we planned the manual way: combing through every past semester one by one to figure out when courses actually run, keeping it all coordinated across spreadsheets and dozens of tabs. The spreadsheet couldn't see prereqs, didn't know about co-op, and validated nothing. We built the tool we needed, and then kept building it for everyone else.
