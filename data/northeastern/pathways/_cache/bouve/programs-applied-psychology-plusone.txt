It is for students seeking entry into a PhD program in an applied psychology discipline or undergraduates who desire quality coursework in graduate-level applied psychology, but do not seek to become licensed mental health counselors.
Gain core competencies in applied psychology fields.
Have a better understanding of how family systems, culture, and economic status play a role in one’s mental health.
Research, analyze, and evaluate key statistics and trends found in behavioral health.
Prepare to work in a variety of settings, such as community mental health agencies, higher education, research settings, non-profit agencies, and within healthcare organizations.

Curriculum Coursework for the Applied Psychology master’s provides evidence-based knowledge in applied psychology.
Students take 10 classes (5 per semester) for 30 credits total, and select one of two tracks that focuses their curriculum:
Child, Adolescent, and Family Psychology Prevention Science Sample Curriculum The below is subject to change.
Please speak with your academic advisor and refer to the program requirements in the University Catalog .
